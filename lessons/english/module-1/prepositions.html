English grammar

Using prepositions well

Small words like at, on and in carry the time, place and direction of a sentence. Learn thirteen working rules, then check yourself with twenty practice questions.

How to use this page

Work through the four steps in order. It takes about twenty minutes, and you can come back at any time.

1

Read the rules

Each rule card names the preposition, says when to use it and gives two examples. Use the colored filters to see one group at a time.

The rules, with examples

The preposition in each example is highlighted. Every rule has two examples.

Showing 13 of 13 rules.

Timeat

Clock times and exact moments

Use at for a precise time or a short, fixed moment: clock times, noon, midnight and night.

  • The train leaves at 6:45.
  • We will meet at noon.
Timeon

Days and dates

Use on for days of the week, calendar dates and named days.

  • My exam is on Monday.
  • She was born on 5 May.
Timein

Longer periods

Use in for months, years, seasons and parts of the day (but at night).

  • It rains heavily in July.
  • I revise in the evening.
Timeforsince

Length of time and starting point

Use for with an amount of time. Use since with the moment something began.

  • I have lived here for ten years.
  • She has been ill since Tuesday.
Timebyuntil

Deadlines and limits

Use by for 'no later than'. Use until for something that continues up to a point.

  • Submit the form by Friday.
  • The shop stays open until 9 pm.
Placeat

Points and addresses

Use at for a specific point, an event or a street address.

  • Wait for me at the bus stop.
  • He lives at 12 Park Road.
Placeon

Surfaces

Use on when something touches a surface, such as a table, wall or floor.

  • The book is on the table.
  • Hang the poster on the wall.
Placein

Enclosed spaces and areas

Use in for rooms, containers, cities and countries.

  • The keys are in the drawer.
  • She works in Kochi.
Movementto

Direction towards a place

Use to for movement towards a destination or a person.

  • We walked to the market.
  • She sent a letter to her uncle.
Movementintoout of

Entering and leaving

Use into to enter a space and out of to leave it.

  • He jumped into the pool.
  • The cat ran out of the room.
Doer, tool and groupbetweenamong

Two things or many

Use between for two people or things. Use among for three or more.

  • Sit between Ravi and Mala.
  • She found a letter among old papers.
Doer, tool and groupbywith

Doer and tool

Use by for who did something. Use with for the tool used or the person who accompanies you.

  • The poem was written by Tagore.
  • She cut the paper with scissors.
Fixed pairsgood atdepend on

Fixed pairings

Many adjectives and verbs need one particular preposition. Learn them as pairs: good at, afraid of, interested in, depend on, listen to.

  • He is good at chess.
  • Farmers depend on the rain.
